- **HN:** the 7-chromatic target is right, but any 7-chromatic unit-distance graph has at least **6,993 vertices** (Parts 2020, Table 2; de Grey–Parts 2023), 13.7× the 509 record. The 509-plus-gadget plan cannot reach it. The 6-chromatic calibration proves only χ ≥ 6, already known (OpenAI 2026); it is calibration, not a decision step.
- **Jacobian:** the config-2 system targets `det J = x²` in `L^(1)=K[x,x⁻¹,y]`; `jac.py` rejects any non-constant determinant. A normalized solution is not a counterexample. The GGHV reduction is one-directional. `(72,108)` is probably dead (wstrinz); `(75,125)` is live, but the likely output is a degree bound, not a counterexample.
- **BSD:** the "certifiable" region `k ≤ 1` is exactly where Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin proves `r = k`. The possible mismatches start at `(4,2)`, not `(2,0)` or `(3,1)`. The branch has no admissible object.
- **Closest:** Jacobian, and only after the reconstruction gap is closed. **Abandon:** BSD as a counterexample hunt. HN is a long shot; keep it small and level-correct.
- The agent gate is a document, not a scheduler. Fix the representation, not the prompt.

## 2. Per problem

### 2.1 Hadwiger–Nelson

**(a) The real obstruction.** The gadget hierarchy is level-indexed, and the repo is at the wrong level:

| level | primitive | what it forces | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| k=3 | diamond (4v/5e) | apices equal | known; Moser spindle gives χ=4 |
| k=4 | de Grey H/L/M | a monochromatic √3-triangle in some copy | known; gives χ=5 |
| k=5 | **virtual edge** (forced-different pair at non-unit distance) | endpoints differ in every 5-colouring | **missing** |

The diamond is a 3-colouring equality gadget; that is why diamond composition saturates at χ=4. The 509 graph is a 5-chromatic graph, i.e. a **k=4 obstruction**; measuring its relations at k=5 is the wrong level and explains the null result. De Grey's H is not a 5-colouring gadget: it has four 4-colourings, two with a monochromatic triple (arXiv:1804.02385 §2); it is L (52 copies) that forces the triple. At 5 colours H has a colouring with no mono √3-triangle (the repo's own `six.py` says so). The plan's "7v/12e wheel forcing a monochromatic √3-triangle at 5 colours" is a misreading.

The 5→6 step, on the known route, is the construction of a **5-colouring virtual edge**. The known 6-chromatic two-distance graphs — Parts' 16/31-vertex graphs with distances 1 and the golden ratio (arXiv:2010.12656), Exoo–Ismailescu's 426-vertex graph (arXiv:1909.13177), and De Neve et al.'s 33-vertex graph with distances 1 and √3 (DOI 10.1080/00029890.2025.2559554) — are waiting for exactly this gadget. Replace their non-unit edges by a unit-distance gadget that forces different colours in every 5-colouring and you have a 6-chromatic unit-distance graph. This is the literature route the repo has not tried. The "virtual edge at level 5" is also the stated wall in the 14-point, three-distance note (github.com/HeliCorgi/fourteen-points-six-colors).

**(b) Three moves, ranked by EV toward a terminal object:**

1. **Build the 5-colouring virtual edge for √3 and compose with the 33-vertex two-distance graph.** Artifact: an explicit 6-chromatic unit-distance graph + DRAT. *On-core? Only for Target 0, not the 7-chromatic terminal object.* It is still the highest-EV HN move because it is the only one with a known target and a single identifiable missing gadget.
2. **Kill the 509-plus-gadget route with the size bound.** Artifact: a corrected plan citing `v₇ ≥ 6993`; any 7-chromatic graph needs ≥ 6,993 vertices, so a ~516-vertex composition is provably 6-colourable. *Off-core as an object, but a decisive kill.* EV: high (saves compute).
3. **Direct 7-chromatic search on a ≥6,993-vertex seed.** Artifact: the terminal object (not-6-colourable graph + DRAT). *On-core.* EV: low; no construction or seed exists at that scale.

### 2.2 Planar Jacobian

**(a) The real obstruction.** Not the 445 unknowns. The GGHV object is a pair in `L^(1)=K[x,x⁻¹,y]` with `[P,Q]=x²`, obtained from a hypothetical polynomial counterexample by automorphisms of `L^(1)` that are **not** automorphisms of `K[x,y]` (arXiv:2204.14178, Prop. 4.3). The implication is one-directional: counterexample ⇒ normalized pair. A solution to the normalized system is **not proved to reconstruct** a polynomial map with `det J = 1` and a collision. The repo's terminal checker makes this explicit: `jac.py` returns `REJECTED` whenever the determinant is not constant. The config-2 system's target is `x²`. Therefore a config-2 solution — the entire J1/J3/J4 program — cannot become a claim without a reconstruction step. This is the reconstruction gap, and it is the actual wall.

Second, the live computation likely outputs a **degree bound**, not a counterexample (speculation). `(72,108)` is probably excluded by wstrinz's Theorem A; `(75,125)` is the smallest open pair, with 445 unknowns in the full lattice. Proving that lattice empty raises the degree bound; it does not produce a Jacobian counterexample. That is a theorem about JC, but not the terminal object in `DEFINITION_OF_SOLVED.md`.

**(b) The literature route not tried.** The **edge cascade / Newton–Puiseux descent** used by GGHV and Moh, not Gröbner on the full lattice. GGHV's proof works with the normal form `ℓ₁,₀(P)=R²`, `ℓ₁,₀(Q)=R³`, `R=x⁴y⁷(y+1)`, and an ODE for the first correction; wstrinz's audit reproduces this. Moh's approximate-root method is how degree 100 was proved (DOI 10.1515/crll.1983.340.140). A 445-unknown Gröbner basis is the wrong algorithm for a determinantal system with Newton-polygon structure.

**(c) Three moves:**

1. **Reconstruction audit (the one thing).** Artifact: `problems/jacobian/reconstruct.py` + a claim file; it inverts the GGHV automorphisms, clears denominators, checks `det J = 1`, and runs `jac.py --claim`. *On-core: it is the only bridge from the normalized system to the terminal object.* EV: highest, because it tests whether the closest branch is even aimed at the target.
2. **Edge cascade on `(75,125)`.** Artifact: a proof of emptiness (degree bound) or a solution plus reconstruction. *On-core only if a solution reconstructs and collides; otherwise a negative result.* EV: medium; the structure is real, but the likely outcome is a bound.
3. **Independent check of wstrinz Theorem A.** Artifact: a counterexample to Theorem A or an independent verification. *On-core if it produces a map; otherwise replication.* EV: low; the repo's own audit already found the argument substantive.

**On "interior points are load-bearing."** The observation is right; the inference is overbroad. The vertices-only system is empty because it deletes the terms that produce the target monomial `x²` — a statement about that truncation, not a theorem that interior points are always load-bearing. A minimal-support model that includes the necessary interior points can be valid. The `(75,125)` "support obstruction" is an artifact of the four-corner ansatz: the full determinant can produce `x²` from interior terms. The correct lesson is that the support of `det J` is the Minkowski difference of the supports, and any truncation must be justified by the Newton-polygon edge/valuation structure (the cascade), not by taking vertices. "Never a valid reduction" is false as stated.

### 2.3 BSD

**(a) The real obstruction.** Gross–Zagier–Kolyvagin: if `r_an(E/Q) ≤ 1`, then `r_an = r_MW` (Jetchev–Lauter–Stein, arXiv:0707.0032, Theorem 1.1, citing Gross–Zagier 1986 and Kolyvagin 1990). The repo's entire "certifiable" region is `k ∈ {0,1}`. That is exactly the region where the theorem says `r = k`. So the shapes `(2,0), (4,0), (3,1), (5,1)` are not merely hard — they are **impossible**. A mismatch requires `k ≥ 2`; parity then gives the smallest possible shape `(4,2)`, then `(5,3)`, `(6,2)`, `(6,4)`, etc.

The second error is the claim that `k ≥ 2` is not certifiable. That is a limitation of the AFE interval method, not a mathematical fact. LMFDB states that for classical modular form L-functions "the displayed analytic rank has been rigorously verified in all cases (even when it is greater than one or the L-function is not self-dual)" (lmfdb.org/L/2/2366/1.1/c1/0/17/Reliability). The method is rigorous zero-counting / interval arithmetic, not a black-box interval around a single value. The repo's "k ≥ 4 is not proved for any curve" is likewise overstated.

**(b) The literature route not tried.** Rigorous zero-counting (argument principle) and modular-symbol special-value computation, as LMFDB does. The AFE estimator is a fine numerical scout; it is not a certificate. The search should target rank-2 curves for the `(4,2)` shape, not `k ≤ 1`.

**(c) Three moves:**

1. **Kill the branch with GZK.** Artifact: a one-page finding + an updated gate; *off-core as a terminal object, but the highest-EV action because it reallocates compute.*
2. **Build a `k ≥ 2` certificate via rigorous zero-counting, calibrate on 389a1 (k=2) and 5077a1 (k=3), then scan rank-2 curves.** Artifact: a certified `(E,r,k)`; *on-core only if `r ≠ k`.* EV: low but nonzero; this is the only terminal move.
3. **Use LMFDB's rigorous analytic ranks as a backend and map the rank-2 coverage.** Artifact: a coverage table; *off-core unless a mismatch appears.* EV: low.

## 3. Where we are wrong

1. **BSD's certifiable region is empty.** `(2,0)` and `(3,1)` are ruled out by GZK (arXiv:0707.0032, Thm. 1.1). The correct smallest target is `(4,2)`. Your parity argument is right but you stopped one theorem too early.
2. **"k ≥ 2 is not certifiable" is false.** LMFDB rigorously verifies analytic ranks >1 for classical modular forms. Your B1 code is honest that it is a heuristic AFE; the plan then promotes that to a mathematical impossibility. It is not.
3. **The Jacobian terminal checker rejects your own target.** `jac.py` requires a constant determinant; the config-2 system sets `det J = x²`. No config-2 solution can pass `accept.py`. The GGHV reduction is necessary, not sufficient; you have no reconstruction.
4. **J3 does not "test Theorem A."** `[1]` on the vertices-only specialization is consistent with Theorem A but says nothing about the full lattice; only a solution would refute it. The plan's "a solution would refute it, `[1]` would agree" is backwards: `[1]` is the expected null result of a weaker system.
5. **The HN gadget is at the wrong level.** De Grey's H has four 4-colourings, two with a mono triple (arXiv:1804.02385 §2); L, not H, forces the triple. The plan calls H a 5-colouring forcing gadget. Your own `six.py` says H has a 5-colouring with no mono triangle. Three files give three different levels (5, 4, 3) for the same object.
6. **The 509-plus-gadget plan cannot reach χ=7.** Any 7-chromatic unit-distance graph has ≥6,993 vertices (arXiv:2010.12668, Table 2; arXiv:2303.14714). A 509-plus-7 composition is ~516 vertices and is provably 6-colourable. The plan's step 5 is dead on size, not on compute.
7. **A 6-chromatic graph does not settle 6-vs-7.** It proves χ ≥ 6, already known since Sept 2026. To settle χ=6 you need an explicit 6-colouring of the plane; to settle χ=7 you need a 7-chromatic graph. The 6-chromatic graph is calibration, not a decision step.
8. **"Interior points are load-bearing" is overgeneralised.** The vertices-only failure is a truncation artifact; the `(75,125)` support obstruction is not a property of the full determinant. The correct statement is about the support of `det J` and the need for the edge cascade, not a general ban on minimal supports.
9. **The admissibility gate is not enforced by the task graph.** J1/J3/J4, H1/H2, and B1 all produce artifacts the terminal checker does not consume. The repo's own gate would reject them. You have diagnosed substitution and then scheduled it.
10. **`accept.py` does not enforce BSD's "proved" clause.** `accept_bsd` only checks method labels and `k ≤ 3`; `python tools/accept.py --problem bsd --claim consulting/bsd_gate_probe.json` exits 0 with `(rank 0, k=2)` and labels `"2-descent"`/`"lcalc"`. A schema label is not a certificate. The gate would accept a numerical rank as proved.

## 4. Agent system

**What breaks at 10+ agents.**

- **Checker serialisation.** The 509 graph's 4-colouring UNSAT takes 134 s. A 6,993-vertex 7-chromatic check will likely be far worse (speculation). Ten agents will queue on one SAT solver; you need a checker farm with content-addressed caching and parallelism.
- **Correlated verifier B.** If B is written by the same model with the same toolchain, it shares the same misconception. The 509 two-implementation check is the model to follow; the rest of the pipeline does not have it. Require a different solver/CAS and, where possible, a different author model.
- **No dependency DAG.** The 3-task queues are FIFO. J3 depends on J1/J2; H1 depends on H2. At 10+ agents, blocked tasks will be launched. Add explicit `depends_on` and a `BLOCKED` state.
- **Artifact IDs are not content-addressed.** `candidates/<id>.json` will collide or go stale. Use SHA-256 of canonical JSON.
- **The tangent tax is gameable.** `tokens / max(1, passes)` rewards cheap repeated passes. A branch that verifies the same graph 100 times has a low tax. Use `tokens / terminal_passes` with a novelty requirement, and a per-problem baseline; the global 20× median is unstable at 10 agents.
- **No negative-result channel.** The most valuable output is "this space is empty" or "this reduction fails," and the schema discards it. Add a first-class `NEGATIVE` artifact with its own checker (infeasibility proof, support obstruction, degree bound). Otherwise the system relearns the same dead ends.

**Over-engineered.** The 10,000-agent scaling table; five roles for a 5–7 agent team; the Breaker/Verifier split; the global 20× kill rule; the ban on progress reports. At `p = 0`, scaling is not the problem.

**Missing.** A reconstruction requirement; a dependency DAG; a content-addressed store; a checker cache; an independent B toolchain; a per-problem baseline for kills.

**Single highest-leverage change.** Require a checked **reconstruction map** from every intermediate artifact to the terminal object. The current gate asks "does the terminal checker consume it?" — but the tasks use intermediate checkers, so the answer is always "no" and the agent substitutes a proxy. A reconstruction map makes the implication explicit and testable: for Jacobian, the inverse GGHV normalisation; for BSD, the GZK theorem (which shows `k ≤ 1` cannot mismatch); for HN, the composition from a level-5 virtual edge to a 6-chromatic graph, and from a 6-chromatic graph to a 7-chromatic one (which fails). If no reconstruction exists, the artifact is off-core by definition. This one change would have caught all three current errors.
