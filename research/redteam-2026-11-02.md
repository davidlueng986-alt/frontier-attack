**Concrete refutation attempt (and a self-caught error).** My first pass applied

## 2. Minimum size of a 5-colour monochromatic-triple gadget

**Claim.** Any unit-distance graph forcing a monochromatic triple in every
5-colouring has at least 11 vertices.  The repo's argument: every 7-vertex UDG
is 4-colourable, and a 4-colouring refines to a 5-colouring with classes of
size `<= 2`; five classes of size 2 cover 10 vertices.

**The repo's argument is not sufficient for n = 8, 9, 10.**  The refinement
step is false for a general 4-colouring of 10 vertices: class sizes `(3,3,2,2)`
split into six classes, not five.  A concrete abstract counterexample to the
refinement lemma is `(K4 - edge) ∨ \bar{K6}`: it is 4-colourable with classes
`(2,1,1,6)` and has no 5-colouring with all classes `<= 2` (the two endpoints
of the missing edge can pair, the other two vertices of the `K4-e` are adjacent
to everything and must be singleton classes, and the six independent vertices
need three classes).  It has 29 edges and is not a unit-distance graph, but it
shows the counting sentence in the repo does not prove the n = 10 case.

**What I actually proved.**  I used three independent attacks.

**(a) No 5-chromatic UDG on <= 10 vertices.**  A 5-chromatic graph contains a
5-critical subgraph `H`, and every 5-critical graph has minimum degree `>= 4`,
so `m(H) >= 2|H|`.  A unit-distance graph on `k` points has at most `f(k)`
unit edges, with the published exact values (OEIS A186705, Alexeev–Mixon–
Parshall) `f(6..10) = 9, 12, 14, 18, 20`.  For `k = 6,7,8`, `f(k) < 2k`.
For `k = 9,10`, equality forces `H` to be 4-regular; Brooks' theorem then gives
`chi(H) <= 4` unless `H = K5`, and `K5` is not a unit-distance graph (no four
points in the plane are pairwise at distance 1, let alone five).  Hence every
UDG on at most 10 vertices is 4-colourable.  (This is stronger than the repo's
7-vertex statement, and it uses `f(9), f(10)`.)

**(b) A K4-free forcing graph needs more edges than a 10-point UDG has.**
A gadget forcing a *specific* triple `T` must satisfy: every partition of the
vertex set into at most five independent sets puts `T` in one block.  I encoded
this exactly as a hitting-set SAT instance over edge variables, with a witness
5-colouring (so the graph is 5-colourable) and a cardinality bound
`sum e <= f(n)`; `research/scratch/redteam/claim2_sat_k4free.py`.  A UDG is
`K4`-free, so I also forbade every `K4`.  Results:

| n | edge cap `f(n)` | K4-free + 5-colourable witness | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | 23 | 404,003 bad partitions, 406,210 clauses | **UNSAT** |
| 10 | 20 | 82,627 bad partitions, 84,177 clauses | **UNSAT** |
| 9 | 18 | 17,147 bad partitions, 18,200 clauses | **UNSAT** |
| 8 | 14 | 3,643 bad partitions, 4,333 clauses | **UNSAT** |
| 7 | 12 | 803 bad partitions | **UNSAT** (even without K4-free) |
| 6 | 9 | 187 bad partitions | **UNSAT** (even without K4-free) |
| 5 | — | singleton partition is a proper 5-colouring with `T` split | no graph can force `T` |

The UNSAT at n = 10 and n = 11 is decisive for **5-colourable** gadgets: no
`K4`-free, 5-colourable graph on 10 or 11 vertices with at most 20 or 23 edges
forces a designated triple.  Since a UDG has at most `f(10) = 20` or
`f(11) = 23` unit edges and no `K4`, no 5-colourable 10- or 11-vertex UDG
gadget exists.  The same argument covers n = 8, 9, and the n <= 7 cases are
even easier.  This is a computer-assisted proof; the only external input is
the exact unit-distance maxima `f(6..11)`, the same kind of input the repo
already used for `f(7)`.

**The true minimum is therefore at least 12 for non-vacuous gadgets, not 11.**
The repo's bound is true but not tight.  I did not settle n = 12: the partition
count for 12 vertices into at most 5 blocks is about 2.08 million, and the
corresponding SAT instance was not run to completion.  So the best statement
proved here is `n >= 12` for 5-colourable gadgets; an explicit 12-vertex gadget
would make 12 sharp, and a 12-vertex UNSAT would push the bound to 13.  A
5-*chromatic* 11-vertex UDG would be a vacuous “gadget” (no proper 5-colouring
to refute); none is known — the smallest known 5-chromatic UDG is 509 vertices
— and the Brooks argument above rules out 5-chromatic UDGs on at most 10
vertices.  The literal claim `>= 11` is therefore safe; the meaningful
(non-vacuous) bound is `>= 12`.

**(c) Direct construction attempts.**  None produced a gadget:
- Moser spindle (7 v, 11 e, 4-chromatic): **zero** `sqrt(3)` triples.
- Golomb graph (10 v, 18 e, 4-chromatic): exactly two `sqrt(3)` triples,
  `(3,5,7)` and `(4,6,8)`, but the proper 4/5-colouring
  `[0,1,2,1,0,2,0,1,2,3]` avoids both.
- de Grey's H (7 v, 12 e): 31 essential 5-colourings, 22 without a mono triple.
- H plus all 20 triples of unit-circle intersection points: no gadget.
- All 220 nine-leaf stars on the 12-clock circle: no gadget.
- 6,751 ten-point subsets of a 4x4 triangular-lattice patch: no gadget.
- 40,000 random 10-point UDGs (7,775 with a `sqrt(3)` triple): no gadget.

**Semantic caveat.**  If “monochromatic triple” means *any* three vertices of
the same colour, the 10-vertex star `K_{1,9}` is a counterexample: a 5-colouring
splits the nine leaves over four colours, so some colour occurs at least three
times.  The leaves can be placed generically so that no three are pairwise at
`sqrt(3)`.  The claim is therefore true only for the geometric `sqrt(3)`-triple
reading used by de Grey and by the repo's own `six.py`; the prose should say so.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** (for the `sqrt(3)`-triple reading, via the K4-free SAT
bound; the repo's stated counting argument is incomplete for n = 8, 9, 10, and
the non-vacuous minimum is in fact `>= 12`, so the stated bound of 11 is valid
but not tight).

## 3. The level table

**Claim.** The level table says: the diamond forces equality at `k = 3` only;
de Grey's H is a `k = 4` gadget; a level-5 virtual edge is the missing
primitive.

**Method.** Exact enumeration of every proper `k`-colouring of the diamond and
of H, followed by the quotient by the graph's symmetry group; H is the centre
plus a regular hexagon of side 1 (de Grey, arXiv:1804.02385, §3).  Files:
`research/scratch/redteam/claim3_h.py`, `claim3_h_orbits.py`.

**Evidence.**

*Diamond.*  At `k = 2` there is no proper colouring; at `k = 3` there is one
essential colouring and the two tips are equal; at `k = 4` and `k = 5` there
are essential colourings with the tips equal and with them different.  So the
diamond forces equality at `k = 3` only.  **Confirmed.**

*H.*  The exact distance spectrum is `d^2 in {1, 3, 4}`; the two `sqrt(3)`
triples are the alternating rim triples `(1,3,5)` and `(2,4,6)`.  Exhaustive
enumeration:

| k | essential colourings (up to colour permutation) | with a mono `sqrt(3)` triple | without |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 (H is not bipartite) | — | — |
| 3 | 1 | **1** | 0 |
| 4 | 11 | 7 | **4** |
| 5 | 31 | 9 | **22** |

Quotienting the 11 essential 4-colourings by the dihedral symmetry `D6` of the
hexagon gives **4 orbits**, of which **2** contain a mono triple and **2** do
not — exactly de Grey's “four essentially distinct 4-colourings, two with a
monochromatic triple and two without”.  In particular **H alone does not force
a monochromatic triple in every 4-colouring**; it is a `k = 3` pattern gadget
(every proper 3-colouring makes both alternating rim triples mono).  The
`k = 4` forcing gadget is **L = 52 copies of H**, not H.

*Virtual edge.*  If a unit-distance gadget forces its two terminals to have
different colours in every proper 5-colouring, then replacing each of the 32
`sqrt(3)` edges of the 33-vertex 6-chromatic graph by that gadget gives a
6-chromatic unit-distance graph: a 5-colouring of the composition would induce
a 5-colouring of the 33-vertex graph with every `sqrt(3)` edge bichromatic,
contradicting its 6-chromaticity.  The sufficiency argument is valid.  Whether
such a gadget exists is **not** established; the repo's search is a bounded
negative, not a proof.  So “level-5 virtual edge is the missing primitive” is a
correct description of a sufficient missing object, not a theorem.

**Verdict: REFUTED** as stated.  The diamond and virtual-edge parts are right,
but “de Grey's H is a `k = 4` gadget” is false in the only forcing sense:
H has proper 4-colourings with no mono triple.  The corrected table is
`k = 3: H` (pattern gadget) and `k = 4: L = 52 copies of H` (forcing gadget).

## 4. Gross–Zagier + Kolyvagin and the `k >= 2` conclusion

**Claim.** `GZK`: analytic rank `<= 1` implies `rank = analytic rank`, so any
BSD counterexample has `k >= 2`.  The question is the exact hypotheses:
modular? semistable? all `E/Q`?

**Method.** Literature check of the precise theorem (not a computation), plus
the repo's own `plan/bsd.md` statement.

**Evidence.**  The clean combined statement is Theorem 1.1 of Jetchev–Lauter–
Stein, arXiv:0707.0032, p. 2:

> **Theorem 1.1 (Gross–Zagier, Kolyvagin).** Let `E/Q` be an elliptic curve
> which satisfies `r_an(E/Q) <= 1`.  Then `r_an(E/Q) = r_MW(E/Q)`.

The proof uses a Heegner discriminant `D`: an imaginary quadratic
`K = Q(sqrt(-D))` in which every prime factor of the conductor `N` splits.
Such a `K` exists for every `E/Q` (prescribe the splitting at the finitely many
primes dividing `N`, then choose a prime discriminant by Dirichlet).  For such
`K`, `L(E/K,s) = L(E,s) L(E, chi_K, s)`; choosing `K` also with
`L(E, chi_K, 1) != 0` makes the order of vanishing at `s = 1` equal to that of
`L(E,s)`.

The two ingredient theorems:

- **Gross–Zagier (1986).**  For `E/Q` modular and `K` imaginary quadratic with
  the Heegner hypothesis, `L'(E/K,1) = c · h(P_K)` with `c != 0`; a first-order
  zero forces the Heegner point `P_K` to have infinite order.
- **Kolyvagin (1989/1990).**  For `E/Q` modular and `K` with the Heegner
  hypothesis, if `ord_{s=1} L(E/K,s) <= 1` then
  `rank E(K) = ord_{s=1} L(E/K,s)` (and `Sha` is finite in the rank-0 case).

**Hypotheses.**  Modularity is required by both ingredient theorems.  It is a
theorem for **all** elliptic curves over `Q`: Wiles (1995) for semistable
curves, Breuil–Conrad–Diamond–Taylor (2001) for all `E/Q`.  Semistability is
therefore **not** a hypothesis of the final theorem; it was only a hypothesis
of the 1995 modularity theorem and is superseded.  The Heegner hypothesis is a
condition on the auxiliary `K`, not on `E`, and is satisfiable for every
`E/Q`.  So the theorem covers all `E/Q`.

**Consequence.**  A rank counterexample (`r_MW != r_an`) must have
`r_an >= 2`, i.e. `k >= 2`.  This is unconditional for the rank part of BSD.

**Caveats.**  (i) The theorem is about the **rank part** of BSD.  A
counterexample to the full BSD leading-coefficient formula could in principle
have `k = 0` or `1`; the repo's terminal object is `(E, r, k)` with `r != k`,
so this is the rank part and the claim is correctly scoped.  (ii) The repo's
“smallest admissible shapes” also uses `r ≡ k (mod 2)`, which is the parity
conjecture, not a theorem; that does not affect `k >= 2`.  (iii) “Kolyutin” in
the task is presumably a typo for Kolyvagin; no separate Kolyutin hypothesis is
needed.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** (rank part; all `E/Q`; modularity automatic, no
semistability).

## 5. The `(75,125)` vertices-only system

**Claim.** The vertices-only system is empty because every monomial of
`det J` has `x`-degree at least 8, so the `x^2` coefficient equation is
`-1 = 0`.

**Method.** Independent SymPy computation from the corner data (no import of
`problems/jacobian/*`): `Delta' = {(0,0),(3,0),(4,1),(0,5)}`,
`N(P) = 3 Delta' = {(0,0),(9,0),(12,3),(0,15)}`,
`N(Q) = 5 Delta' = {(0,0),(15,0),(20,5),(0,25)}`,
`P = a0 + a1 x^9 + a2 x^12 y^3 + a3 y^15`,
`Q = b0 + b1 x^15 + b2 x^20 y^5 + b3 y^25`.
File: `research/scratch/redteam/claim56_jacobian.py`.

**Evidence.**  `det J = P_x Q_y - P_y Q_x` has exactly six monomials:

```
(8,24), (11,27), (14,14), (19,19), (26,2), (28,4)
```

The minimum `x`-degree is **8**; `x^2` is **not** present; the coefficient of
`x^2` in `det J` is **0**.  The system `det J = x^2` therefore has 7 coefficient
equations (six from `det J` plus `x^2`), and the equation at `(2,0)` is
literally `-1`.  Eight unknowns, seven equations, one of them `-1 = 0`:
inconsistent over any field (in characteristic 2 it is `1 = 0`).  This holds
for every specialization of the eight coefficients, including cases where some
coefficients vanish, because specializing can only remove monomials, never add
`x^2`.

**Scope.**  This is the minimal-support/vertices-only specialization.  The
full-lattice system of `config75.py` does contain `x^2` and is not excluded by
this argument; the repo says so itself.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

## 6. Edge 3 of the `(75,125)` cascade

**Claim.** Edge 3 of the cascade is vacuous because it is horizontal and
`det J` is identically zero on it.

**Method.** Re-derived the four edge leading forms from the corner data with
generic edge coefficients (`claim56_jacobian.py`):
- edge 1, `w = (1,1)`, top: `P_e = a2 x^12 y^3 + a3 y^15`,
  `Q_e = b2 x^20 y^5 + b3 y^25`;
- edge 2, `w = (1,-1)`, diagonal: `P_e = a1 x^9 + a2 x^12 y^3`,
  `Q_e = b1 x^15 + b2 x^20 y^5`;
- edge 3, `w = (0,-1)`, horizontal: `P_e = sum_{i=0}^9 c_i x^i`,
  `Q_e = sum_{j=0}^15 d_j x^j`;
- edge 4, `w = (-1,0)`, vertical: `P_e = sum_{i=0}^15 c_i y^i`,
  `Q_e = sum_{j=0}^25 d_j y^j`.

**Evidence.**  For edge 3 both `P_e` and `Q_e` are univariate in `x`, so
`det J(P_e,Q_e) = P_e'(x)·0 - 0·Q_e'(x) = 0` identically, for **all**
coefficients.  The same is true of the vertical edge 4.  The top and diagonal
edges are **not** identically zero.  The cascade JSON agrees:
`descent[3]` has `normal [0,-1]`, `vacuous: true`,
`leading_form_identically_zero: true`, `equations: 0`.

**Index caveat.**  There is an index collision worth recording: the
`edges_NP` array numbers the left vertical edge as index 3, while the descent
order numbers the bottom horizontal edge as edge 3.  Both are vacuous
(vertical is also univariate in one variable), so the claim is true under
either reading, but “edge 3” is ambiguous between the two tables.

**What vacuity does and does not mean.**  A zero leading form means the edge
imposes no condition at that filtration level; the next non-vacuous condition
is at lower filtration order and involves interior coefficients, outside the
repo's 3-edge cascade.  Vacuous is not the same as “the full system is
satisfiable”; the repo's verdict (`DESCENDED 3 EDGES`, filter not solution)
correctly does not claim that.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** (with the index caveat).

## 7. The 33-vertex two-distance graph

**Claim.** The graph in `runs/two_distance.json` is 6-chromatic, with exactly
32 `sqrt(3)` edges and no third edge length.

**Method.** Independent exact distance check in `Q(sqrt(3), sqrt(5))`
(`claim7_two_distance.py`), plus two independent SAT encodings (one-hot and
2-bit binary) and three solvers per encoding.  The edge list was compared with

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| vertices | **33** |
| edges | **113** |

| edges with `d^2 = 3` | **32** |

one-hot encoding, written independently, gave the same verdicts.  No
counterexample survived.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

The load-bearing negative result is #3: the repo's own “level error” correction
is still off by one for H.  H is a `k = 3` gadget (it forces a mono
`sqrt(3)`-triple in every 3-colouring), and L is the `k = 4` forcer.  The
practical consequence is that a search for a “k = 4 H-like gadget” of 7
vertices is searching for the wrong object; the smallest 4-colour pattern
forcer in this family is the 121-vertex L.

The load-bearing positive results that the repo stated too weakly are #2 and
#4.  #2 is true, but the stated counting argument only covers 7 vertices; the
K4-free SAT bound is what actually covers 10 and 11, and it shows the
non-vacuous minimum is at least 12, so the repo's 11 is a valid but non-tight
lower bound.
#4 is true for all `E/Q`, but only because modularity is now a theorem; the
original Gross–Zagier/Kolyvagin statements are modularity-conditional, and
semistability is not needed.
